How to read the map

Each district on the interactive map is shaded by the party of the member elected there, and by whether that member is new.

Colors

Dark red: Republican, incumbent (I)
Light red: Republican, newly elected (N)
Dark blue: Democrat, incumbent (I)
Light blue: Democrat, newly elected (N)
★ Gold star: member holds a leadership position

Using the map

  1. Choose State House or State Senate.
  2. Click any district, or pick one from the list, to see its member, party, status, leadership role and counties served.
  3. Switch to Counties and pick a county to see every legislator whose district includes it.
  4. Use Highlight members to show only leadership, only new members, or one party.
  5. Each member's card also shows their Capitol office, committees and filed bills, updated nightly from official sources.
  6. Download a one-page PDF handout for any district or county.

How the Missouri legislature works

The Missouri General Assembly has two chambers. A bill must pass both in identical form before it goes to the governor.

Missouri House

163 members, each representing roughly 38,000 people. Representatives serve two-year terms, and every seat is on the ballot in each even-numbered year. Members may serve up to four terms (eight years) in the House.

Missouri Senate

34 members serving four-year terms. Elections are staggered: even-numbered districts were on the ballot in 2026, and odd-numbered districts are next elected in 2028. Senators may serve up to two terms (eight years).

Legislative session

The regular session begins in early January and runs to mid-May. Lawmakers debate bills, pass the state budget and can override a governor's veto with a two-thirds vote in each chamber.

Majority leadership

The majority party elects the Speaker of the House and the Senate President Pro Tem, who run each chamber. Majority floor leaders decide which bills come up for debate.

Minority leadership

Minority floor leaders speak for the smaller party, organize its members and negotiate with the majority on legislation.

Caucus & budget roles

Whips count votes, caucus chairs and secretaries organize each party's members, and budget chairs lead work on the state budget. See all leaders →
